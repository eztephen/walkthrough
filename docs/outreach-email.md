# Outreach — property managers

Three pieces: a first email, one follow-up, and a short version for LinkedIn. Send the first, wait four working days, send the follow-up once, then stop.

Everything in `{braces}` must be filled in by hand for each agency. Emails that skip that step read like a mail merge, and that's how they get treated.

---

## First email

**Subject** — pick one and keep it for the whole batch so you can tell which works:

- `Your routine inspection reports`
- `The evening after inspection day`
- `Quick question about owner reports at {Agency}`

---

Hi {First name},

I noticed {Agency} looks after {a specific, true detail — "around 300 rentals across the north shore", "the Riverton and Kingsway portfolios"}. That usually means a lot of routine inspections, and a lot of evenings turning phone photos into owner reports.

I've built a small tool for that part. You walk the property on your phone, tap a condition for each room and take photos as you go. By the time you're back at the car, the owner report is written — rooms, photos, maintenance items and quote requests to your tradespeople.

It runs in the browser, so there's nothing to install. Here's a live demo with a sample property already loaded: {demo link}

Would you be open to fifteen minutes next week? I'll hand you my phone, you inspect your own office, and you'll have the finished report before the coffee's gone cold. If it doesn't save you time, I'll say so and leave you alone.

{Your name}
{Phone} · {Website}

<sub>You're getting this because {Agency}'s address is published for business enquiries. Reply "no thanks" and I won't email again.</sub>

## Follow-up — four working days later, same thread

**Subject:** `Re: {original subject}`

---

Hi {First name},

One question, and then I'll stop: how long does a routine report take your team, from walking in the door to the owner getting it?

If the answer is more than an hour, the demo is worth fifteen minutes — reply with a time that suits. If it isn't, no more emails from me.

{Your name}

## Before you send

**Personalise the first line, every time.** Two minutes on the agency's website — portfolio size, suburbs, a recent listing — is the difference between a reply and the bin. Never guess a number you can't see.

**Send from your own domain**, as plain text, one at a time. Ten to twenty a day. No tracking pixels, no attachments, no HTML templates.

**Keep a simple sheet**: agency, person, date sent, follow-up date, reply. You're measuring which subject line and which opening line get replies — that only works if you write it down.

**Don't buy lists.** Use addresses the agency publishes for business enquiries.

**Anti-spam law applies to cold email.** New Zealand's Unsolicited Electronic Messages Act 2007 and Australia's Spam Act 2003 both require you to identify yourself accurately and include a working way to opt out, and both limit who you can email without consent. A published business address used for a message relevant to that business is commonly treated as inferred consent — but check the current rules for the country you're emailing before your first batch. This note is not legal advice.

**Don't claim what you haven't earned.** No "trusted by agencies" until an agency trusts you. No time-saved figures you haven't measured. The demo's own timer is your evidence — let it make the claim.
